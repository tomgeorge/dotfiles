# Writing clearly and concisely

Apply Strunk's *The Elements of Style*, and avoid the patterns that make prose read as AI-generated.

## Strunk's rules
Usage:
1. Form the possessive singular by adding 's.
2. In a series of three or more, use a comma after each term except the last.
3. Enclose parenthetic expressions between commas.
4. Put a comma before a conjunction that introduces an independent clause.
5. Don't join independent clauses with a comma.
6. Don't break sentences in two.
7. A participial phrase at the start of a sentence must refer to the grammatical subject.

Composition:
8. One paragraph per topic.
9. Begin each paragraph with a topic sentence.
10. **Use the active voice.**
11. **Put statements in positive form.**
12. **Use definite, specific, concrete language.**
13. **Omit needless words.**
14. Avoid a succession of loose sentences.
15. Express coordinate ideas in similar form.
16. **Keep related words together.**
17. Keep to one tense in summaries.
18. **Place emphatic words at the end of the sentence.**

When a rule's application is unclear, read its section in `elements-of-style/`. `03-elementary-principles-of-composition.md` covers rules 8–18 and is usually enough. `02-elementary-rules-of-usage.md` covers rules 1–7, `04-a-few-matters-of-form.md` headings and quotations, and `05-words-and-expressions-commonly-misused.md` word choice.

## AI patterns to avoid
- **Puffery:** pivotal, crucial, vital, testament, enduring legacy.
- **Empty "-ing" phrases:** ensuring reliability, showcasing features, highlighting capabilities.
- **Promotional adjectives:** groundbreaking, seamless, robust, cutting-edge.
- **Overused vocabulary:** delve, leverage, multifaceted, foster, realm, tapestry.
- **Formatting overuse:** excessive bullets, emoji, bold on every other phrase.

Be specific, not grandiose: say what the thing does. `signs-of-ai-writing.md` (Wikipedia's field guide for spotting AI-written text) has the full catalog, for when you're editing text that reads as generated.
